# Construct Validity Brief Design

## Purpose

Create a concise Chinese memo for discussing HEART-Bench's construct-validity issue with the user's advisor. The memo should clarify the mismatch between the paper's current “human-like psychology” claims and the benchmark's actual operationalization without presenting the issue as irreparable.

## Audience and Tone

The primary audience is the user's academic advisor. The tone should be formal, objective, and constructive. The memo should distinguish evidence-supported claims from unsupported extrapolation and frame the recommended changes as a defensible repositioning of the paper.

## Scope and Structure

The final memo should fit approximately two A4 pages. The first page should define the central issue, summarize the strongest supporting evidence, and explain the likely reviewer concern. The second page should present a staged solution: immediate claim and terminology revisions, followed by optional experiments that would strengthen construct validity. The conclusion should state the recommended paper positioning and include a concise English operational definition suitable for direct reuse.

## Deliverables

Create `CONSTRUCT_VALIDITY_BRIEF.md` in the workspace root and `output/pdf/CONSTRUCT_VALIDITY_BRIEF.pdf`. The PDF should preserve Chinese and English typography, use clear section hierarchy, and avoid clipped text, broken tables, or raw LaTeX.

## Content Constraints

The brief should retain only evidence necessary to establish the construct mismatch. It should cover synthetic personas and memories, expert-adjudicated persona references rather than observed human behavior, possible shortcut signals, and the mismatch between a single-turn MCQ and broad agent-psychology claims. Recommendations should distinguish low-cost manuscript revisions from stronger empirical additions. Detailed line-by-line revision instructions, extensive experiment inventories, and exhaustive validity taxonomy should be omitted.

## Acceptance Criteria

The memo is successful if a reader can understand within two pages what the benchmark actually measures, why the current framing may invite construct-validity criticism, and which changes can address the concern before submission. The Markdown and PDF must contain the same substantive content, and every PDF page must render legibly with no missing glyphs.
